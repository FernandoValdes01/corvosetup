# Design Taste

Use these questions as verifiable design constraints, not as a request to “make it beautiful.”

- **Hierarchy:** Is the primary action or information obvious within seconds?
- **Spacing:** Does a small, consistent spacing scale create rhythm and grouping?
- **Typography:** Are size, weight, line length, and contrast limited and readable?
- **Alignment:** Do related elements share clear edges and structure?
- **Density:** Can users scan the screen without unrelated information competing?
- **Color:** Does color communicate state, action, or hierarchy rather than decoration?
- **Components:** Do equivalent elements look and behave equivalently?
- **Interactions:** Are hover, focus, active, selected, disabled, loading, and error states coherent?
- **Responsive:** Does hierarchy survive narrower viewports without overflow or awkward reordering?
- **Accessibility:** Are semantics, keyboard behavior, focus visibility, labels, and contrast reasonable?
- **Consistency:** Does the screen visibly belong to the existing product?
- **Restraint:** Can anything be removed without losing function or comprehension?
- **Professionalism:** Would this remain comfortable and credible in daily use?

Prefer one clear visual idea, quiet surfaces, intentional whitespace, and a restrained accent. Use borders, elevation, rounding, animation, and gradients only when they explain hierarchy or interaction. Evaluate the rendered result, then correct what fails these questions.
